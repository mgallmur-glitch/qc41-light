## Three mistakes

### M1 — The seller presented the dashboard before establishing the prospect's current process and decision criteria.
- Evidence: “Let me show you the dashboard.”
- Consequence: The demonstration began without a diagnosed problem, making the later feature list feel generic.

### M2 — The seller answered the previous adoption failure with an unsupported superiority claim instead of diagnosing why adoption failed.
- Evidence: “This is different because our AI is much better.”
- Consequence: The prospect's implementation risk remained unresolved before price was introduced.

### M3 — The seller accepted an undefined document review without agreeing on what the prospect would evaluate or when they would decide.
- Evidence: “Great, I will send the deck.”
- Consequence: The call ended without a specific follow-up commitment, owner, date or decision criterion.

## Three corrections

### Fix for M1
Delay the demonstration and ask how the current CRM and follow-up process work before selecting what to show.

**Example:** “Before I show anything, how are leads moving through your CRM today, and where does follow-up break?”

### Fix for M2
Clarify the previous implementation failure, including what the team resisted and what successful adoption would require.

**Example:** “What caused the team to stop using the last tool: setup, workflow friction, training, or something else?”

### Fix for M3
Define the review criteria and schedule a specific follow-up only if both parties have a legitimate reason to continue.

**Example:** “What do you need to confirm in the deck, and should we review those points together Thursday at 2?”

## Recovery line

> Before we talk about a discount, can we go back to what failed last time and define what your team would need to adopt this successfully?

It moves the conversation away from premature price pressure and back to the unresolved implementation risk.
